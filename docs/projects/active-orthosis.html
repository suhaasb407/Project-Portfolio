(04) ASME Biomechanics · Rehabilitation

Active orthosis

A motorized upper-limb brace for rehabilitation, shaped around a 3D scan of a real arm.

Role
Mechanical & electrical design
Organization
ASME Biomechanics, Purdue
Timeline
Jan — May 2026
Tools
Fusion 360, KiCad, 3D scanning, 3D printing
01

Overview

Patients recovering from injury or stroke often need repetitive, assisted elbow motion to rebuild strength. An active orthosis adds a motor to the brace so it can drive or assist that motion. I designed and manufactured components for the orthosis in Fusion 360 and built its control electronics in KiCad.

  • Mechanical: iterative CAD and dimensional tolerancing to improve manufacturability and range of motion.
  • Electrical: KiCad schematics and PCB layouts, with sensors, motor drivers and power circuitry chosen for reliable actuator control.
  • Integration: joined the hardware with the embedded control system and fixed the mechanical/electrical compatibility issues found in testing.
02

Scan-to-CAD

A brace that doesn't fit doesn't get worn. Instead of modeling a generic tube, we started from a 3D scan of a real arm and used it as reference geometry, so the upper-arm and forearm shells follow the actual contours of the limb, including where the elbow axis really sits.

03

Mechanical design

The orthosis is two contoured shells, one for the upper arm and one for the forearm, joined at an elbow hub that houses the actuator and its bearing stack. Large cut-outs reduce weight and let the skin breathe without weakening the load path from the hub into each cuff.

04

Electronics & validation

I designed the KiCad schematics and PCB layouts for the control board and selected the sensors, motor driver and power circuitry. Validation meant putting the mechanical hardware and embedded controls together on the real prototype, then working through the problems that only appear at that stage: connector clearances, wiring routes through the shells, and driver behavior under load.
